# Power Factor and kVA Allocation — Level 2 EV Chargers on a 75 kVA, 600–208 V Transformer

**Scope:** A dedicated 75 kVA 600–208 V transformer feeding a 208 V panel with 10 × 40 A breakers, each serving one Level 2 EVSE. The transformer exists solely to serve the EV load.

All figures in this document come from a single model, defined in §3. They are generated by the accompanying Rust program `ev_site_load.rs`, which is the authoritative source — the constants in §2 and §3 are its `const` declarations, and every table below is its output, **with one exception: the per-phase loading table in §8.2**, which is computed separately because the program models aggregate site load only and carries no panel configuration. Where earlier working notes gave different numbers, the values here supersede them (see §9).

**Precision note.** Figures are quoted to two decimals for traceability against the program, not because the underlying data supports that accuracy. Transformer loss and excitation values are typical for the class, not measured nameplate data, so treat the third significant figure as indicative.

---

## 1. Power factor of the charger itself

**Key distinction:** the Level 2 EVSE on the wall is a contactor plus a J1772 pilot signal. It does not rectify or condition power. **Power factor is set by the vehicle's onboard charger**, so "the charger's PF" is really "the car's PF."

Modern onboard chargers use active power factor correction (PFC), so they behave nearly as a resistive load at full output.

| Operating point | Typical true PF | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Full rated current (32 A / ~6.6 kW) | 0.98 – 0.995 | Active PFC; THDi typically 3–8% |
| ~50% of rated | 0.96 – 0.98 | PFC still in continuous conduction |
| ~20% of rated | 0.88 – 0.95 | Fixed losses and EMI filter capacitance start to matter |
| Taper / end of charge (<1 kW) | 0.6 – 0.9, often **leading** | EMI filter X-capacitors dominate; small kW, so small absolute impact |

**Design value used throughout this document: PF = 0.99 at full current.**

Relevant standards and program requirements:
- SAE J2894/1 and most utility EV programs specify PF ≥ 0.95 above roughly half load.
- Current-generation EVs comfortably exceed this. Older PHEVs with 3.3 kW chargers sit at the lower end (~0.95–0.97).

**The non-unity portion is mostly distortion, not displacement.** This matters twice over: power factor correction capacitors cannot correct it, and it does not add arithmetically with the transformer's reactive power — it adds in quadrature (§3).

## 2. Per-vehicle electrical quantities

The load is **current-limited, not power-limited**. The pilot signal caps vehicle current at 32 A (40 A breaker × 80% for continuous load, CEC Rule 8-104). Apparent power therefore follows directly from voltage and current:

$$S_{EV} = V \times I = 208 \times 32 = \mathbf{6.66\ kVA}$$

Power factor determines how much of that 6.66 kVA appears as real power. **It never inflates the kVA.** This is why `kW ÷ PF` is the wrong derivation for this load type — it implies current is free to grow as PF degrades, which the pilot signal prevents.

Decomposing 6.66 kVA at PF 0.99:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Apparent power, S | 6.66 kVA |
| Real power, P | 6.59 kW |
| Displacement reactive, Q | 0.89 kvar |
| Distortion reactive, D | 0.30 kvar |
| True power factor | 0.99 |

**Voltage tolerance is the dominant uncertainty**, not power factor:

| Case | Per-EV kVA |
|---|---|
| Nominal 208 V, 32 A | 6.66 |
| Service at +5% (218 V), 32 A | 6.99 |

If a bound is needed for one vehicle in isolation, **7.0 kVA** covers nominal plus full voltage tolerance. Derive it from V × I, not from kW ÷ PF, so it remains correct if breaker size changes. Note that at 40 A continuous EVSE on 50 A breakers the equivalent figures become 8.32 kVA nominal and 8.74 kVA at +5%.

## 3. Transformer model

Typical values for a 75 kVA dry-type unit. Bold entries are declared constants; the reactive figures are products of a per-unit constant and the rating:

| Parameter | Value | Scales with |
|---|---|---|
| Magnetizing current, 2% pu → 1.5 kvar | *derived* | **Nothing** — constant while energized |
| No-load (core) loss | **0.35 kW** | **Nothing** — constant while energized |
| Full-load load loss (copper) | **1.6 kW** at rated | Loading² |
| Impedance ≈ 4.5%, X ≈ 4% pu → 3.0 kvar | *derived* | Loading² |

### 3.1 Formulas

Stated symbolically (see [3.2 Symbol reference](#32-symbol-reference) below), so that every quantity traces to a declared constant. No numeric literals appear below; §3.2 maps each symbol to its program identifier and value.

**Per vehicle** — apparent power is set by current, then resolved into components:

$$S_{EV} = \frac{V \cdot I_b \cdot k}{1000} \qquad S_1 = \frac{S_{EV}}{\sqrt{1 + THD^2}}$$

$$P_{EV} = S_{EV} \cdot PF \qquad D_{EV} = S_1 \cdot THD \qquad Q_{EV} = \sqrt{S_1^2 - P_{EV}^2}$$

**Site**, for `N` vehicles at full current, with loading fraction $x = N \cdot S_{EV} / S_{rated}$:

$$P = N \cdot P_{EV} + P_{NL} + P_{LL} \cdot x^2$$

$$Q = N \cdot Q_{EV} + m \cdot S_{rated} + X \cdot S_{rated} \cdot x^2$$

$$D = N \cdot D_{EV}$$

$$S = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2 + D^2} \qquad PF_{true} = P / S$$

The three components are orthogonal, so they combine in quadrature rather than by simple addition.

**Constraint.** $PF \le 1/\sqrt{1 + THD^2}$. True power factor is the product of displacement power factor and the distortion factor, so the distortion factor is a ceiling reached only at unity displacement. At `THD = 0.045` the ceiling is 0.99899, leaving 0.90% headroom above `PF = 0.99`. The program asserts this rather than clamping, so incompatible constants fail loudly instead of silently yielding $Q_{EV} = 0$.

### 3.3 Energised but idle

With the transformer live and no vehicle charging:

$$S_{standing} = \sqrt{0.35^2 + 1.5^2} = \mathbf{1.54\ kVA} \quad (PF \approx 0.23)$$

This is the **standing block**. It is present at 3 a.m. with nothing plugged in, and is caused by no individual vehicle.

**Shape of the curve.** PF rises steeply from 1 to 3 vehicles, then flattens at ~0.98. Two opposing effects cancel: the fixed 1.5 kvar of magnetizing current shrinks as a *fraction* of load, while leakage kvar grows with loading². Beyond roughly 30% loading, site PF barely moves.

**Low PF at one vehicle is not a problem.** At N=1 the site draws only 7.4 kVA. A poor ratio applied to a small quantity produces a small absolute penalty.

## 5. Allocating kVA per EV

### The core difficulty

The transformer's contribution splits into two parts that behave completely differently:

| Component | Magnitude | Scales with |
|---|---|---|
| Excitation (magnetizing kvar + core loss) | 1.5 kvar, 0.35 kW | Nothing |
| Leakage kvar + load loss | 3.0 kvar, 1.6 kW at full load | N² |
| EV load itself | 6.656 kVA each | N |

**There is no physically correct way to divide a fixed quantity among a variable number of users.** Any single per-EV number embeds an assumption about how many vehicles are charging. The first question is therefore not "what is the per-EV kVA" but "at what loading level am I allocating?"

### The two allocation bases

| EVs | Fully allocated (S/N) | Marginal (S(N) − S(N−1)) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 7.37 | 5.82 |
| 2 | 7.00 | 6.64 |
| 3 | 6.91 | 6.72 |
| 4 | 6.87 | 6.76 |
| 5 | 6.86 | 6.80 |
| 6 | 6.85 | 6.83 |
| 7 | 6.85 | 6.86 |
| 8 | 6.86 | 6.90 |
| 9 | 6.87 | 6.93 |
| 10 | **6.88** | 6.97 |

Marginal values are measured against the energised-but-idle state (1.54 kVA), which is why the first vehicle's marginal figure (5.83 kVA) is *below* its own 6.66 kVA: its near-unity-PF current partially rotates the standing magnetizing current toward the real axis. This is a genuine vector effect, not an error.

The fully allocated figure has a **shallow minimum near N = 6** (6.85 kVA). Below that, fixed excitation dominates; above it, leakage kvar takes over. Both ends of the range sit near 6.9.

### Recommended method: two-part allocation

Mirror the physics rather than forcing everything into one number.

| Block | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Standing block** | **1.54 kVA** (0.35 kW + 1.5 kvar) | Attributed to the EV *system*, not to any vehicle |
| **Per-EV block** | **6.82 kVA** | Site total at design build-out, net of the standing block, divided by 10 |

The per-EV block is derived by removing the standing P and Q from the N=10 totals and re-taking the magnitude: √(67.15² + 11.26² + 2.99²) / 10 = 6.82 kVA.

This is defensible because it does not pretend the excitation is caused by any particular vehicle, and it is stable — it does not swing depending on how many cars happen to be plugged in when you look.

### If a single blended number is required

**6.9 kVA per EV at design maximum.** This is the honest fully-allocated figure at build-out (6.88, rounded), and it sits within 1.5% of the marginal cost of the 10th vehicle (6.97). The same number therefore serves both "what did the fleet cost in aggregate" and "what did the last vehicle added cost."

Note that this equivalence holds only *within* the 10-stall design. It does not extend to an 11th vehicle — see §8.

## 6. Which basis for which purpose

| Purpose | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Utility service capacity / demand submission | **Site total: 68.77 kVA at build-out** | Never build up from per-EV × N — that re-introduces the double-count of the fixed block |
| Tenant / department cost recovery | **Two-part (§5)** | Standing block goes to common area or splits by parking stall count, including unoccupied stalls |
| Evaluating the cost of the last stall added | **Marginal: 6.97 kVA** | Valid only up to the 10th stall; beyond that see §8 |
| Single blended figure for a report | **6.9 kVA/EV** | Valid only at or near design build-out |

**Add to the building total vectorially, not by scalar addition.** Sum P and Q separately across the whole service, then take the magnitude once. If the rest of the building runs 0.88 lagging and the EV block runs 0.98, arithmetic addition of apparent powers overstates the combined kVA by roughly 1–2%, because |S₁ + S₂| ≤ |S₁| + |S₂| with equality only when phase angles match. Scalar addition is conservative and therefore safe for sizing, but should not be used for billing, where it over-charges the EV load.

## 7. Practical implications

**Utility billing.** Most Ontario LDCs apply a power factor adjustment below 0.90. The site is above that in every charging scenario. The only sub-0.90 condition is the energised-idle state, which at 1.54 kVA carries negligible demand consequence.

**Do not install power factor correction capacitors.** With PF ≥ 0.94 in all charging states there is nothing meaningful to gain, and capacitors near a bank of PFC front-ends risk harmonic resonance with the transformer's leakage inductance.

**Harmonic diversity works in your favour.** Identical chargers do not produce perfectly in-phase harmonics. Measured aggregate THDi at the transformer is typically *lower* than any single vehicle's — often 3–5% at full site load. The distortion column in §4 is therefore conservative.

**The scenario that actually degrades PF is not many EVs — it is simultaneous end-of-charge taper.** Several vehicles drawing under 1 kW each, with EMI filter capacitance producing leading current, can pull site PF into the 0.85–0.95 range. The exact figure is highly manufacturer-dependent and is **not modelled here**; treat it as an observation about what a spot meter reading at 5 a.m. might show, not as a design number. It is brief and low-consequence because the associated kVA is small.

## 8. Capacity and code notes

### 8.1 Is the transformer correctly sized?

**Yes, for the stated design load.** 10 × 6.656 kVA = 66.56 kVA of vehicle load, 68.77 kVA including the transformer's own contribution, against a 75 kVA nameplate — **91.7%**.

Continuous operation at that level is legitimate. The 80% continuous-load derating familiar from branch circuits applies to **conductors and overcurrent devices** (CEC Rule 8-104), *not* to transformers. Dry-type transformers are rated for continuous output at nameplate, and IEEE C57.91 treats continuous rated loading as consistent with normal expected life.

**But there is no margin.** "Correctly sized" and "has headroom" are different claims, and only the first holds here. There is no diversity allowance, EV charging is continuous duty by definition, and the conditions in §8.2 and §8.3 can each consume what little cushion exists. Unless load management is in use (EVEMS per CEC Rule 8-500 and Section 86), this transformer is fully committed.

### 8.2 Per-phase loading — the aggregate figure conceals an imbalance

**This is the item most likely to cause a real problem, and the 91.7% figure hides it.**

Level 2 EVSE at 208 V is a single-phase line-to-line load. Ten such loads cannot be distributed evenly across three phase pairs, so per-phase loading always exceeds the aggregate. Worst-case line current for representative breaker distributions, at 32 A per stall and 208.2 A rated line current:

| Breaker split (AB / BC / CA) | Worst line current | % of rated | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 4 / 3 / 3 | 194.6 A | 93.5% | Acceptable |
| 3 / 3 / 4 | 194.6 A | 93.5% | Acceptable |
| 5 / 3 / 2 | 224.0 A | **107.6%** | **Over nameplate** |

A 5/3/2 distribution overloads one phase by roughly 8% while the aggregate site draw still reads a comfortable 91.7%. This is a realistic field error rather than a hypothetical: two-pole breakers get relocated during construction, and nothing in the total site demand reveals the problem.

**A 4/3/3 split is required, not merely preferred. Verify the as-built phase assignment.**

*Computed separately from `ev_site_load.rs`, which deliberately models only aggregate site load and carries no panel configuration.*

### 8.3 Conditions that erode the remaining margin

- **Ambient temperature.** The 75 kVA rating assumes roughly 30 °C average / 40 °C maximum ambient. A poorly ventilated electrical room, or an uncooled parking structure in summer, reduces the effective rating. At 93.5% worst-phase loading there is little to give.
- **Future EVSE upgrade.** Rebuilding any stall for 48 A EVSE on 60 A breakers breaks the arithmetic immediately.
- **Voltage drop.** Roughly 4% through 4.5% transformer impedance at this loading, stacking with feeder drop. Partly self-correcting, since vehicles draw constant current and lower voltage therefore means lower kVA, but confirm the panel stays within tolerance.

### 8.4 What would restore margin

**EVEMS** (CEC Rule 8-500, Section 86). Capping aggregate site draw preserves all 10 stalls while committing materially less than 66.56 kVA, buying back both growth headroom and the phase-imbalance cushion. Where dwell time greatly exceeds charging time — overnight residential or fleet charging — the user-visible impact is typically negligible.

### 8.5 Expansion limits

**The design is capped at 10 vehicles by two independent limits**, and no figure in this document should be extrapolated past that point:

1. **Panel:** 10 breakers, so there is no 11th circuit.
2. **Transformer:** an 11th vehicle would reach 75.76 kVA against a 75 kVA nameplate.

Because both limits bind at once, expanding the site means a larger panel *and* a larger transformer. The incremental cost of stall 11 is therefore a step change in equipment, not a marginal 7 kVA — the smooth marginal curve in §5 does not continue past stall 10, and the allocation figures in this document would have to be recomputed for the new transformer's loss and excitation characteristics.

### 8.6 Harmonic derating

Dry-type transformers derate under harmonic loading, but with ten PFC front-ends at 3–8% THDi the K-factor demand is low (K-1 to K-4). This is a minor effect, but confirm against the nameplate.

### 8.7 These figures are not a code load calculation

For CEC Section 8 and Section 86 purposes, the calculated load is the connected EVSE rating (or the EVEMS-limited maximum), and transformer losses do not enter it at all. The 6.9 kVA figure is an **energy and capacity accounting** number for the 600 V side — appropriate for utility demand, cost allocation, and upstream capacity planning. Do not substitute it into a load calculation form.

## 10. Summary

1. The vehicle's onboard charger, not the EVSE, sets power factor. Design value **0.99** at full current.
2. Per-EV apparent power is **6.66 kVA**, fixed by V × I. Use **7.0 kVA** if voltage tolerance must be covered.
3. Site power factor is **0.944 at one vehicle**, rising to a **0.98 plateau from three vehicles upward**.
4. The transformer's fixed excitation (**1.54 kVA**) belongs to the system, not to any vehicle. Allocate it separately.
5. Recommended allocation: **1.54 kVA standing + 6.82 kVA per vehicle**, or **6.9 kVA per vehicle** blended at design build-out.
6. Report site capacity as the **site total (68.77 kVA)**, never as per-EV × N.
7. The transformer is **correctly sized but has no margin** at 91.7% continuous.
8. **Verify the breakers are split 4/3/3 across phase pairs.** A 5/3/2 split overloads one phase to 107.6% while the aggregate figure still looks healthy (§8.2).
